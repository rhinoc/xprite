# Anatomy and pose

A sprite with head, chest and hips stacked on one vertical and both arms hanging parallel reads as a mannequin, however well it is shaded. At 16–32 px the pose is nearly the only thing the player reads, and the pose comes from three things: a line of action, where the weight sits, and which way the ribcage and pelvis tilt against each other. Anatomy detail is the last 10 %; it only matters up to the few landmarks that survive at your size.

## Rules

1. **Draw the line of action first.** One 1-px curve on its own layer, before any mass: **C** for effort, recoil, bracing; **S** for relaxed, cocky, contrapposto; **straight diagonal** for reach, dash, lunge. Head, spine and the leading limb sit on it; the curve ends on the support foot. At 16–32 px it is often all the player reads. Templates `anatomy-loa-guides-32` (the bare curves) and `anatomy-loa-poses-32` (the same curves on dummies).
2. **Three line types only: straight, C, S.** Pair curves *asymmetrically*: offset the left and right bulge of a limb or torso by 1–2 px along its length. Mirrored or parallel curves close the form and look like a snowman.
3. **Build from three hard masses and three soft links.** Hard: head, ribcage, pelvis. Soft: neck, waist, hip joint. Keep the alternation. Ribcage and pelvis tilt against each other: at 32 px slope the shoulder line 1–2 px one way and the hip line 1–2 px the opposite way, and the figure is no longer a mannequin.
4. **Decide where the weight lives before drawing.** One foot, both feet, seat, knees, hands. The vertical through the support foot is the line of gravity. If head, chest and hip centres all sit within 1 px of it the pose is **static**; shift each 1–3 px, alternating sides, for energy. A wide regular base reads stable; a thin or irregular base reads in motion.
5. **The loaded side pinches, the free side stretches.** Weight on one leg raises the pelvis on that side and tips the ribcage the other way (contrapposto). At 32 px+ make the loaded flank 2–3 px shorter between ribs and hip with one notch pixel; make the free flank one long unbroken diagonal.
6. **Motion leans: the top is ahead of the base.** Draw a faint line from the planted foot through the pelvis to the head; walk ≈ 1–3 px of head offset per 28 px of height, run and dash far more (angles are [INFERENCE]; Loomis gives no numbers). The push foot stays behind that line; when the torso leans forward the rear leg extends to counter it. Template `anatomy-poses-motion-32` (dash).
7. **Profile spine is an S.** Head forward of the ribcage, ribcage tipped back, pelvis forward. Standing side view: chest slightly back and up, hips slightly forward, belt tilted forward. A vertical stack of head / chest / hips is the mannequin. Template `anatomy-poses-balance-32` (side stand).
8. **Posture is character.** Lead with a body part: chest = hero, head = scholar or menace, pelvis = slob, knees = coward. Shoulder height is a one-pixel emotion dial: up 1 px = tense, down 1 px = defeated. Compact, narrow, head below the shoulder line = fear or defeat; wide stance with an upward triangle = heroic; low horizontal mass = relaxed; a 30–60° diagonal axis = action.
9. **Limbs taper and bend.** 3 px at the root to 2 px at the end; never parallel sides, never a right angle, never a mitred elbow. A 1-px curved cap at elbow, knee or wrist shows which way the limb faces: round = toward the viewer, flat = side-on.
10. **Overlaps are T-junctions.** The near form's outline runs on; the far form's outline stops against it. Never let two outlines cross. Keep ≥ 1 px of background between an arm and the torso it crosses, or merge them on purpose.
11. **Keep only the landmarks that survive.** ≤ 32 px: shoulder point, elbow / knee bend, ankle. Use inside / outside asymmetry: calf bulge high on the outside, ankle bone high on the inside, a 1 px offset between the two outlines. At 32–64 px only 3–4 muscle shapes read: chest fan, shoulder cap, ribs + ab block, forearm bulge near the elbow. Everything else is shading.
12. **Volume is constant.** Bone length never changes; foreshorten by overlap, not by shrinking. A stretched pose is 10–20 % longer and thinner, a crouch shorter and thicker, and the pixel count of the body stays about the same (squash and stretch).
13. **Twist with two blocks.** Shift the chest block and the pelvis block horizontally by *different* amounts (chest +2 px, hips −1 px at 32) and shear the shoulder line against the hip line. That one move sells a turn.
14. **Pose the extremes and the counter-move.** Action is readable at the start or end of a sweep, not the middle. Anticipation is a spine curve opposite to the action, then the reverse C (`44-attacks-and-impacts`).
15. **Profile reads fastest.** If the action must be read instantly, show it in profile; front and 3/4 views hide limbs and flatten the waist (`37-views-and-directions`).
16. **Construct in order:** line of action → three masses → limbs (tubes) → hands and feet → outline → shading. One layer each, merged last. Make 2–3 thumbnails at 8–16 px high first and choose; the first try is never the best.

## By size

| canvas | what the pose can carry |
|---|---|
| 8 | One idea: lean (head 1 px off the feet) or crouch. No limbs to tilt. |
| 16 | Head offset 1 px; shoulder / hip tilt 1 px; arms 1 px. Three readable poses: stand, lean, crouch. |
| 24 | Contrapposto as a 1-px tilt; bent knee; arm swing; no pinch notch. |
| 32 | Full contrapposto, pinch notch, S spine in profile, chest / hip block twist, elbow and knee bends. |
| 48–64 | Ribcage and pelvis as separate shapes, wrapping-line arcs on limbs, 3–4 muscle planes, shoulder girdle tilt. |

## Procedure

1. `addLayer` "line-of-action"; draw the curve with `applyBatch` `stroke` or paste a guide template. Decide the weight foot and mark its column.
2. `addLayer` "dummy"; block head, chest and pelvis as three flat shapes on the curve with `applyBatch` `grid` or `applyBatch` `ellipse` / `rectangle`. Tilt shoulders against hips (rule 3).
3. Add limbs as 3→2 px tapered strokes (`applyBatch` `line`, or `grid`). Hands and feet as clusters.
4. `render`; then the silhouette test: `duplicateLayer`, `replaceColor` every colour with one dark flat colour, `render`, then delete the copy. If the action is unclear change the pose, not the shading.
5. Shift a mass by `applyBatch` `blit` to the new x/y plus `clear` of the old one, rather than redrawing; compare variants on separate frames with `render` `onionSkin`.
6. Only then outline and shade (`02-shading-and-light`). For animation keep the same masses across frames (`06-layers-and-rigging`, `42-walk-and-run`).

## Mistakes

- Stiff statue → centres of head / chest / hip on one vertical, shoulder and hip lines parallel → rule 3–4: tilt and shift.
- Pose reads on one side only, other limbs lost → arm swallowed by the torso → leave ≥ 1 px gap or move the limb off the body.
- Limbs like pipes → constant width, no bend → taper and give 1 px of bend.
- Leaning figure looks like it will fall over → no counter-weight, base too narrow → plant the push foot behind the line and extend the rear leg, or move the mass over the foot.
- Symmetrical "fly-swatted" front pose → both arms spread flat → hide one limb behind the body, turn the head, drop one shoulder.
- Pose changes figure size between frames → volume drift → keep masses fixed, move them rigidly (rule 12).
- Muscle noise at 32 px → every group drawn → keep four shapes, shade the rest.

## Review

- A line of action (C, S or diagonal) is visible on the silhouette alone.
- Shoulder line and hip line are not parallel (unless the pose is deliberately frontal and static).
- The weight foot is identifiable and under the centre of mass, or the lean is clearly intended.
- Every limb tapers and no two outlines cross.
- Filled flat, the pose still says what the character is doing.
- Body pixel count is stable across poses of the same character.
